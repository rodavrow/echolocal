Wake-word detection runs on the device. Home Assistant processes speech and replies through
the selected Assist pipeline.

## Two assistants

**Assistant 1** and **Assistant 2** each have their own wake word, Assist pipeline, sensitivity,
sounds and timing. Assign different pipelines to the two wake words, including local and cloud pipelines.
A slot with no wake word is off.

## Set it up

<Steps>

1. In Home Assistant, open the device's **Assistant 1** device.

2. Pick an **Assistant** (Assist pipeline) and a **Wake word**. Custom wake words added to Home
   Assistant are listed too. The [EchoLocal integration](/start/integration/) adds a wake word library for managing them.

3. Repeat for **Assistant 2** to configure the second wake word and pipeline.

</Steps>

On an Echo Show you can also pick the wake word under **Settings → Voice assistants**. That needs
**Allow the device to perform Home Assistant actions** turned on for the device in the ESPHome
integration; without it the row is dimmed.

## More wake words

Turn these on under **Settings → Voice assistants** on an Echo Show to add
more choices to each assistant's **Wake word** list:

| Toggle | Adds |
|---|---|
| **Amazon wake words** | Alexa, Amazon, Computer, Echo, Ziggy, Hey Disney, Hey Melissa, Hey Samuel, Hey Shaq and Amit Ji, in several languages and dialects: **Pryon: Alexa**, **Pryon: Alexa (de-DE)** |
| **Gemini wake word** | "Hey Google", listed as **Gemini: Hey Google** |

A word is downloaded the first time an assistant uses it.

## Starting without the wake word

- **Echo Dot:** tap the action button for assistant 1, hold it for assistant 2.
- **Echo Show:** **Talk now** on the assistant's settings page.
- From Home Assistant: the **Wake** button on the assistant's device (`wake_assistant_1`,
  `wake_assistant_2`).

## On the screen

On the Echo Shows,
**Settings → Voice assistants** has a page per assistant:

- **Talk now**
- **Wake word**
- **Wake word sensitivity**: higher wakes more easily, with more false wakes. In Home Assistant the
  same setting is a threshold (0.5–0.99, default 0.85) where *lower* wakes more easily
- **Wake sound**: a chime, a [custom tone](/features/tones/), or none
- **Response playback**: **Stream** starts sooner and can stutter on a slow network; **Download
  first** waits for the whole answer
- **Visuals**: a visual for listening, thinking and replying, in the panel or full screen

## Timing, follow-up and recordings

Find these in Home Assistant on each assistant's device, in the
[EchoLocal card](/start/integration/#the-card)'s assistant panel, or in the device's
[web page](/use/web/):

| Setting | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Follow-up time** | Keep listening after a reply, so you can ask again without the wake word (0–30 s) | 0 |
| **Max listening time** | Stop listening after this long (5–60 s) | 15 s |
| **Max thinking time** | Timeout for a Home Assistant response (5–300 s) | 90 s |
| **Reply buffer** | How much of a streamed reply to collect before playing (0–3000 ms) | 650 ms |
| **Recordings kept** | Keep the audio of the last N requests (0–10), for the integration's Activity tab | 0 |

The **Last wake word**, **Last heard** and **Last reply** sensors show the most recent turn.

On the Echo Dot, the ring shows each stage with its own effect: **Wake word effect**, **Thinking
effect** and **Replying effect**, per assistant. See [Echo Dot: buttons and light
ring](/use/dot/).

## Voice match

Voice match tells who said the wake word. It needs the [EchoLocal integration](/start/integration/),
which keeps every voice and sends them to all your devices, so a voice recorded once is known
everywhere. The speaker model is downloaded the first time voice match is used.

### Add a voice

On an Echo Show, open **Settings → Voice
assistants → Voice match** and tap **Add a voice**, or use the integration's
[Voices tab](/start/integration/#voices), which also works with an Echo Dot. Pick a Home Assistant
person, or **Someone else** and type a name.

<Steps>

1. Say the wake word, then the phrase on the screen. This is repeated five times, with a different
   phrase each time.

2. Say it once more to confirm. If the last one doesn't match the first five, you see **That didn't
   sound like the same voice**; record again.

3. If the new voice sounds very like a voice already saved, you're asked whether to keep it.

</Steps>

While recording, wake words go to the recording, not to an assistant.

### Each voice

Tap a voice to record it again, remove it, or set its **Match threshold** (0.30–0.70, default
0.50). Higher needs a closer match: fewer turns credited to the wrong person, more turns where
it's left as **Unknown**.

### Where it shows

- The **Last speaker** sensor (`last_speaker`) names who woke the device, or **Unknown**;
  **Last speaker score** (`last_speaker_score`) is how close the match was.
- The integration's [Activity tab](/start/integration/#activity) shows the speaker on each turn.

### Recognized voices only

Turn on **Recognized voices only** on an assistant's page on an Echo Show, or
`known_voices_N` in Home Assistant, and that assistant answers only voices it recognizes:

- The wake chime plays as usual, but nothing goes to Home Assistant until the voice is matched,
  about 3 seconds after the wake word plus the time the match takes.
- A voice it doesn't recognize ends the turn with the error sound and look. Home Assistant never
  sees it.
- With no voices saved, every wake word is refused.
- Follow-up questions after a reply, **Talk now**, the action button
  and the **Wake** button skip the check.

## Announcements from Home Assistant

Home Assistant can make the device speak without a wake word: `assist_satellite.announce`,
`assist_satellite.start_conversation`, or a text-to-speech announcement on the media player
(`tts.speak` or `media_player.play_media` with `announce: true`). The device shows it like a
reply: the assistant's Responding visual with the text on an Echo Show, the **Replying effect**
on an Echo Dot's ring. It starts with the speech, not with the chime before it, and ends with it.

- An announcement that arrives during a conversation waits for the conversation to end.
- A wake word interrupts an announcement, and **"stop"** ends it.
- A media player announcement shows no text. Only speech is shown: a chime or a sound effect
  played with `announce: true` is not.
- `start_conversation` listens as the first assistant afterwards.

The first assistant's look is used. To have another one speak, set **Next announcement
assistant** just before the announcement. It counts for one announcement and then goes back to
`Default`; if no announcement comes, it goes back after 30 seconds.

```yaml
- action: select.select_option
  target:
    entity_id: select.<device>_next_announcement_assistant
  data:
    option: Assistant 2
- action: assist_satellite.announce
  target:
    entity_id: assist_satellite.<device>_assist_satellite
  data:
    message: Dinner is ready
```

Turn **Animate announcements** off, under **Settings → Voice assistants** or with
`switch.<device>_animate_announcements`, and announcements only play, as they did before.

## Avatar mode

On an Echo Show, an assistant can be shown and voiced by a
[LiveTalking](https://github.com/lipku/LiveTalking) avatar on your network. In avatar mode the
avatar's video takes the place of the assistant's look, in the panel or across the screen, and
the avatar speaks the reply with its lips in time. The voice still comes out of the Show's
speaker, so **"stop"** and the wake word interrupt it as usual. Captions are unchanged. The Echo
Dot has no avatar mode.

Avatar mode is set from Home Assistant, normally by the Voice Assistant Manager integration,
with these entities on the assistant's sub-device. `N` is 1 or 2.

| Entity | What it does |
|---|---|
| `switch.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_mode` | Avatar mode on or off |
| `text.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_url` | The LiveTalking address, `http://host:port`, optionally with a path, with no slash at the end |
| `text.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_id` | Which avatar to use; empty uses the server's default |
| `select.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_connection` | **Connect when woken** (default) or **Always connected** |
| `sensor.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_session` | The Show's current LiveTalking session (diagnostic) |
| `sensor.<device>_assistant_N_avatar_state` | `off`, `connecting`, `connected` or `failed` (diagnostic) |

On the Show itself, `switch.<device>_avatar_hold` keeps the avatar that last spoke on screen
between announcements, idle and without captions, instead of going back to the screen before.
The Voice Assistant Manager turns it on for a chat between assistants. A touch, a call, camera
or video, or the avatar disconnecting lets the avatar go, and the next announcement is held
again. The switch is off at every start and turns itself off a minute after the last
announcement.

- **Connect when woken** opens the avatar when the wake word is heard, while you are still
  talking, and closes it about ten seconds after the reply. It also opens as soon as **Next
  announcement assistant** names the assistant. **Always connected** keeps it open, so the
  avatar is there at once.
- An assistant in avatar mode takes the whole reply before it speaks, even when its reply
  delivery is set to stream, because LiveTalking speaks a file.
- While the avatar's picture is coming up, for up to a second, the Show shows the captions
  only. The usual look shows if the picture takes longer, while a call, a camera, the doorbell
  or a video (cast, AirPlay, YouTube or your own) is on the screen, and for the rest of a turn
  if the avatar's picture stalls. The photo screen, music and other apps do not hide the avatar.
- If the avatar is not connected, does not take the reply, or makes no sound within a few
  seconds, the Show speaks the reply itself. A reply is never lost to the avatar.
- Announcements go through the avatar only while **Animate announcements** is on, and the chime
  before an announcement plays on the Show. A media player announcement (`tts.speak` or
  `announce: true`) always plays on the Show with the usual look.
- The Show reaches LiveTalking directly: it needs TCP to the server's port (8010 by default)
  and UDP both ways for the video. If the Show is on another network or VLAN, allow both, or
  **Avatar state** goes from `connecting` to `failed`.

## Stop

Say **"stop"**, no wake word needed, tap the screen on an Echo Show, or tap the action button on an Echo Dot. It stops, in order: a ringing alarm or timer, the assistant, an announcement, then pauses music.

**Stop word sensitivity** in Home Assistant sets the detection threshold (0.5–1, default 0.7).
Set it to 1 to disable stop-word detection.

**Amazon stop word**, under **Settings → Voice assistants**, adds Amazon's "stop" detector next to
the built-in one. It can be turned on only while **Amazon wake words** is on.

## Voice triggers

Two kinds of things you can say, each with its own switches under **Settings → Voice triggers** on
an Echo Show and on the **voice triggers** device in Home Assistant. Everything is on to start
with.

**Actions** are said to an assistant, after the wake word. Assist hands them to the device:

- **"Call the kitchen"**, **"video call the kitchen"**: [an intercom call](/features/intercom/#calling-by-voice)
- **"Broadcast"**, **"broadcast dinner is ready"**: [a broadcast](/features/intercom/#broadcasts)

Actions need the [EchoLocal integration](/start/integration/). When it's missing or out of date,
the Voice triggers page says so, with a code to scan that opens it in HACS.

**Commands** are heard on the device itself, without a wake word (Echo Show):

- **"Radar"** (or "show me the weather radar") opens the [Radar app](/features/apps/#radar)
- **"Mirror"** opens [Mirror](/features/apps/#mirror)

**All actions** and **All commands** turn a whole kind off; each one has its own switch too.

## Alarm and call controls

These actions are spoken after the wake word and need the companion Home Assistant integration.
They also work on the Echo Dot.

| Say | Does |
|---|---|
| "Set an alarm for seven thirty AM" | Saves an enabled one-time alarm |
| "Set an alarm called Work for seven thirty AM every weekday" | Saves a named repeating alarm |
| "List my alarms" | Reads the saved alarms, including disabled ones |
| "When is my next alarm?" | Reads the next enabled alarm and when it will ring |
| "Disable the Work alarm", "enable the Work alarm" | Turns that saved alarm off or on |
| "Delete the Work alarm", "delete the seven thirty AM alarm" | Deletes the matching saved alarm |
| "Snooze", "snooze my alarm" | Snoozes the ringing alarm for its saved snooze duration |
| "Stop my alarm", "dismiss the alarm" | Stops the current alarm without deleting its schedule |
| "Answer the call" | Answers the incoming call with audio only |
| "Answer the video call" | Answers with video when the incoming call supports it |
| "Hang up", "end the call", "decline the call" | Ends, cancels or declines the current call |

Use AM/PM or a 24-hour time such as `19:30`. A one-time alarm rings at the next occurrence of that
time. Repeats accept **every weekday**, **every weekend**, **every day**, or named weekdays such as
**on Monday and Friday**. New alarms have a five-minute snooze. Change their sound, snooze and other
options in the alarm editor.

Existing alarm names or identical schedules are not overwritten. Alarm changes match a saved name
or time; if more than one matches, use its name. **Next** also works: "delete the next alarm".

Each action family has its own switch under **Voice triggers**: **Manage alarms**, **Answer a call**
and **Hang up a call**. To use voice hang-up during a connected call, turn off
**Pause wake word during calls**. With it on, use the screen, buttons or HA instead.

Home Assistant can also call `esphome.<node>_call_answer` with `video: false` for audio-only or
`video: true` for video, and `esphome.<node>_call_hangup` with no arguments. These act on the current
call, including calls between devices.
